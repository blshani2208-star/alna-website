What delays the transition from intake to a decision on next steps?

Summary

CHAPTER 01
What was foundThe reviewed documents did not explicitly define who is responsible for closing the handoff, and some records lack documentation of case receipt. Information does not move consistently. The transition between assessment and the decision on next steps is a possible point of delay; this needs confirmation using timing data between stages.
Main possible explanationUncertainty about who is responsible for the transfer, what information must accompany it and when it counts as complete. This is a hypothesis supported by the findings, not a causal conclusion.
What remains openHow much of the delay reflects increased workload and insufficient capacity, and how much reflects unclear responsibility. Response times from external referrers were not examined.
REPORT STATUS · ACTION DIRECTION FOR EXAMINATION

There is a basis for examining a limited action direction. More capacity information is needed before deciding on a wider scope. The report distinguishes this status from “more information needed” and “insufficient basis for a recommendation.” It does not record an organizational approval.

PROPOSED NEXT STEP FOR DISCUSSION

Consider examining a named responsible role and acknowledgment of receipt at each transfer between assessment and the decision on next steps in one team, while completing capacity data by program.

Three Main Findings

CHAPTER 03

1. Responsibility for closing the handoff is not explicitly defined

OPERATIONAL DATA · DOCUMENT

Observed: The reviewed documents did not explicitly define the responsible role; the procedure assigns the transition to “the team.” In 27 of 68 records, receipt of the case is not documented.

Source 5Source 3

Meaning: The transition may depend on individual initiative rather than an explicit definition.

Still unknown: Whether missing documentation reflects a transfer that did not happen, or one that happened but was not recorded.

2. Information does not move consistently

DOCUMENT · AGGREGATE SUMMARY

Observed: In 4 of 10 cases reviewed internally by the organization, the receiving role sought further clarification of information. The assessment form has no field for the agreed next step. ALNA received only an aggregate summary, not the individual case files.

Source 6Source 4

Meaning: Missing information may contribute to repeated work and delay.

Still unknown: Whether the gap reflects the absence of a template or insufficient time to complete it.

3. Staff report workload pressure and manual tracking

STAFF REPORTS · OPERATIONAL DATA

Observed: 6 of 9 frontline respondents maintain personal tracking outside the system; 3 of 9 indicated that responsibility for the transition is clear. Cases per coordinator rose from 22 to 31 between December 2025 and May 2026.

Source 1Source 7

Meaning: Some workload may stem from unclear responsibility, and some from an actual increase in work volume.

Still unknown: The relative contribution of each.

Evidence types

Operational data shows what was recorded. Documents show how the process is intended to work. Staff reports reflect perceptions and experience; they do not measure what happened in each case.

Protecting respondents

The middle-management and leadership questionnaires each received fewer than five responses, so their results are not displayed. They were used only to check whether themes found in other sources also appeared there.

Main Possible Explanation

CHAPTER 04

Hypothesis: The findings raise the possibility that unclear transfer responsibility, required information and confirmation of completion contribute to reliance on personal initiative and memory. This explanation needs to be examined alongside workload and capacity. It is not a proven cause.

WHAT SUPPORTS IT
  • Receipt is not documented in 27 of 68 records. Source 5
  • The procedure does not explicitly define a responsible role for the transition. Source 3
  • Further information clarification after assessment. Source 6
  • Most frontline respondents report personal tracking. Source 1
WHAT CHALLENGES IT OR SUPPORTS AN ALTERNATIVE
  • Workload and capacity: cases per coordinator increased over the period. Source 7
  • To examine: is there a wait for placement?
  • To examine: does coordination with the service user contribute to delay?
MISSING INFORMATION
  • Timing between stages, to confirm the point of delay
  • Availability by program and role
  • Response times from external referrers
  • Variation between teams
  • Whether missing documentation indicates a transfer that did not happen

Action Options for Examination

CHAPTER 05

Three options based on the findings. They are not ranked and may be combined. The decision is made with the organization; no option is recorded as selected or approved.

A · A responsible role and acknowledgment of receipt at every transfer

Link to finding and explanationFinding 1 · Directly addresses the main hypothesis
Conditions for carrying it outA leading role, one team and an agreed deadline for acknowledgment
Workload and resourcesA brief action at every transfer, without procurement
RisksTechnical acknowledgment without substance; workload shifted to the receiving role
Needed for a decisionWho leads, in which team, and how the receiving role's workload is checked

B · A short information checklist accompanying the case

Link to finding and explanationFinding 2
Conditions for carrying it outAgreement on essential fields and adaptation to the existing form
Workload and resourcesPreparing a template and completing it at every transfer
RisksAn additional form that adds burden and is not completed
Needed for a decisionWhich fields are essential and who approves the template

C · A weekly review of cases that have not moved forward

Link to finding and explanationFindings 1 and 3
Conditions for carrying it outAn up-to-date list and a regular weekly review time
Workload and resourcesRegular weekly time for the program coordinator
RisksThe review may be perceived as monitoring individual staff
Needed for a decisionWho coordinates it, and whether the review remains at team level only

Required before expansion

Availability data by program and role. Without it, we cannot know whether an action focused on clarity would be sufficient; expansion to additional teams should therefore not be recommended.

Not proposed at this stage

A new tracking system or automation. The findings suggest possible lack of clarity in the definition of, and responsibility for, the transition. A tool managing an undefined process could entrench that lack of clarity.

Conclusions and Limitations

CHAPTER 06
WHAT CAN BE SAID
  • The documents did not explicitly define who closes the handoff or what information accompanies it.
  • The transition between assessment and decision is a possible point of delay.
  • There is a basis for examining a limited, testable action direction.
WHAT CANNOT YET BE CONCLUDED
  • That unclear responsibility caused the delay. This is a supported hypothesis.
  • That delay is concentrated at this point, before checking timing between stages.
  • The relative contribution of insufficient capacity and unclear responsibility.
  • That cases without documentation were not transferred.
  • What service users experienced. Direct feedback was not collected.

How to read the report

A finding is what was observed, with a source. A possible explanation is a hypothesis. An action direction is a proposal to examine. A decision is made by the organization.

Group results are displayed only when there are at least five responses. Smaller groups have no percentages, quotations or detailed breakdowns.

“Not measured” is not zero. Missing documentation is not proof that an action did not happen.

Missing information is stated alongside its limit on inference. If it is essential to a decision, the process must not proceed as though it had been received.

Sample report. The organization, numbers and descriptions are simulated and written solely for illustration. They are not client outcomes or proof of functionality already operating in the system. There is no personal or medical information in this report.
