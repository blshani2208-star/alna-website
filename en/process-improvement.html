Service Process Diagnostic and Improvement

ALNA helps care, rehabilitation, and human service organizations understand what is holding a process back, shape tailored recommendations based on the possible mechanism, and check what changed as a result.

What a service diagnostic means at ALNA

Many service organizations already know where something is breaking down: a wait time keeps growing, people disengage between stages, or a team is stretched thin. The harder question is why it is happening and what is worth doing. A standard organizational assessment often provides a snapshot. ALNA continues into examining mechanisms, comparing action alternatives, and checking what happens during implementation.

The process combines existing operational data with the knowledge held by leadership and frontline teams, to build one coherent picture of what's happening in the process, instead of dozens of separate reports.

Bringing documents, data, and perspectives together in a structured diagnostic process is meant to save hours of manual collection, follow-up, and analysis, and to focus professional time where judgment is actually needed.

How it starts, and how the process is built

We start with one service, unit, or process where the organization has a clear question it wants to understand. There's no need to replace a system or launch a broad organizational initiative.

1

Understanding the service pathway: the journey through the process and the finding that indicates where delivery is breaking down.

2

Testing what may explain it, several competing mechanism hypotheses, and the supporting and conflicting evidence for each.

3

Choosing what is worth trying: several action alternatives, compared by fit with the mechanism, the evidence, and the organization's capacity. Recommendations are developed jointly by ALNA and the analyst supporting the process. They are never generated as an automatic decision. Read the full process →

4

Implementing and checking, whether the change was implemented, whether behavior and process shifted, and what can be learned going forward.

A process improvement matters when it helps a person move forward

Progress looks different in every organization: continuity of care, a return to functioning, entry into employment, independence, stability, or expanded opportunity. ALNA starts from the outcome the organization wants to advance and connects the diagnostic process, the selected action, and the measurement to it. An initial implementation period can test whether the action is feasible, whether the target behavior changed, and whether an early process signal is visible. It cannot establish a long-term social outcome on its own.

The Focused Diagnosis

The diagnosis focuses on one service process and one central challenge, not a full organizational review.

Existing information and perspectives

Documents and data already held by the organization, alongside the perspectives of frontline staff, mid-level managers, and leadership.

Possible mechanisms and evidence

Examining several possible drivers of the breakdown, gathering supporting and conflicting evidence, and comparing action alternatives.

Leading recommendation

Alternatives ranked by fit with the likely mechanism, the evidence, and feasibility, down to one leading recommendation.

The focused diagnostic is designed to take 7 to 10 business days, subject to receiving the required materials and responses from the organization.

Implementation & Learning

A good recommendation begins the implementation and learning phase. The support is tailored to the selected action and the organization's needs.

Planning the change

Planning the change together with the organization and the team, including human and technological adjustments and defined target behavior and metrics.

Feasibility & acceptance check

An early check on whether the change fits real working conditions, and what support or training would help it take hold.

Measurement & decision

Measuring implementation, behavior, process, and outcome, and, based on that, a shared decision to continue, adjust, expand, or stop.

Who this is for

Mental health and care services, rehabilitation and employment services, social care and community services, nonprofits, and social programs. Frequently asked questions

Is this different from a standard organizational consulting engagement?

Yes. A general organizational assessment usually stops at a snapshot. ALNA continues into examining possible mechanisms, comparing action alternatives, and checking what changed during implementation.

How long does it take?

The focused diagnostic is designed to take 7 to 10 business days after receiving the required materials and responses. A full process that includes preparation, data collection, feedback, and review can take several weeks.

Is a process not moving the way you planned?

In a short introductory call, we'll understand what's already known, what's still unclear, and whether a focused diagnosis could help.
