Diagnosis & Process Improvement for Care and Human Service Organizations

ALNA helps care, rehabilitation, and human service organizations understand what is holding a process back, shape tailored recommendations based on the possible mechanism, and check what changed as a result.

What diagnosis-and-improvement means at ALNA

Many service organizations already have a sense of where something breaks down — a wait time that keeps growing, drop-off between stages, a team that's stretched thin. The harder question is why it's happening, and what's worth doing about it. A "classic" organizational assessment usually stops at a snapshot; ALNA continues from there into testing mechanisms, weighing response alternatives, and checking what actually happens.

The process combines existing operational data with the knowledge held by leadership and frontline teams, to build one coherent picture of what's happening in the process — instead of dozens of separate reports.

Bringing documents, data, and perspectives together in a structured diagnostic process is meant to save hours of manual collection, follow-up, and analysis, and to focus professional time where judgment is actually needed.

How it starts, and how the process is built

We start with one service, unit, or process where the organization has a clear question it wants to understand. There's no need to replace a system or launch a broad organizational initiative.

1

Understanding the breakdown — the person's journey through the process, and the finding that points to the problem.

2

Testing what may explain it — several competing mechanism hypotheses, and the supporting and conflicting evidence for each.

3

Choosing what's worth trying — several response alternatives, ranked by fit with the mechanism, the evidence, and the organization's capacity. In the pilot, recommendations are built jointly by ALNA and the analyst who supports the process — not automatically. Read the full process →

4

Implementing and checking — whether the change was implemented, whether behavior and process shifted, and what can be learned going forward.

A process improvement matters when it helps a person move forward

Progress looks different in every organization — continuity of care, a return to functioning, entry into employment, independence, stability, or expanded opportunity. ALNA starts from the outcome the organization wants to advance, and connects the diagnosis, the response, and the measurement to it. A short pilot mainly tests implementation, behavior change, and a near-term process outcome — not long-term social mobility on its own.

The Focused Diagnosis

The diagnosis focuses on one service process and one central challenge — not a full organizational review.

Existing information and perspectives

Documents and data already held by the organization, alongside the perspectives of frontline staff, mid-level managers, and leadership.

Possible mechanisms and evidence

Examining several possible drivers of the breakdown, gathering supporting and conflicting evidence, and comparing response alternatives.

Leading recommendation

Alternatives ranked by fit with the likely mechanism, the evidence, and feasibility, down to one leading recommendation.

The focused diagnosis is designed to take 7–10 business days, subject to receiving the required materials and responses from the organization.

Implementation & Learning

A good recommendation is a starting point, not the end of the process. After the diagnosis, you can move into an implementation-and-learning phase, tailored to the chosen response and the organization's needs.

Planning the change

Planning the change together with the organization and the team, including human and technological adjustments and defined target behavior and metrics.

Feasibility & acceptance check

An early check on whether the change fits real working conditions, and what support or training would help it take hold.

Measurement & decision

Measuring implementation, behavior, process, and outcome — and, based on that, a shared decision to continue, adjust, expand, or stop.

Who this is for

Care and mental health organizations, rehabilitation and employment services, welfare and community services, nonprofits and social programs — anywhere complex processes, multidisciplinary work, and decision-making affect the quality of care, rehabilitation, or wellbeing. Frequently asked questions

Is this different from a standard organizational consulting engagement?

Yes. A general organizational assessment usually stops at a snapshot. ALNA continues into testing possible mechanisms, ranking response alternatives, and checking in practice what changed.

How long does it take?

The focused diagnosis is designed to take 7–10 business days, after receiving the required materials and responses. A full process that includes preparation, data collection, feedback, and a wrap-up can take several weeks.

Is a process not moving the way you planned?

In a short introductory call, we'll understand what's already known, what's still unclear, and whether a focused diagnosis could help.
